# Project MO — Development Log

**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Session:** Goal 4 — Seated Legs and Static Arms Completion

## Objectives

* Refine the simplified seated-leg geometry
* Create removable mounting interfaces for both legs
* Model and mount the two static arms
* Complete the external-parts stage of the enclosure

## Work Completed

* Refined the seated legs so they extend 5.5 mm beyond the enclosure while remaining beneath the body
* Maintained the revised 13 mm total leg depth using an 8 mm main section and 5 mm foot section
* Applied 2 mm fillets to soften the principal external leg edges
* Verified that both legs remain cosmetic and do not replace the silicone feet as the enclosure's primary supports
* Created removable square leg-mounting interfaces
* Used 5 × 5 × 2.5 mm pegs with clearance-adjusted sockets approximately 2.7 mm deep
* Verified that both legs have no volumetric interference with the main body outside their intended contact faces
* Used the original arm mesh as a visual reference for the hand profile and overall pose
* Modeled one simplified hand and arm, with the hand resting on the supporting surface
* Improved the hand-to-arm transition by removing the initial hand fillet before creating the lofted arm geometry
* Created a 6 × 6 × 3 mm arm peg and matching clearance-adjusted socket through the enclosure side wall
* Mirrored the completed arm across the body's center plane to create the opposite side
* Verified symmetry and completed interference checks for both arms
* Saved the arm and leg designs as separate reusable Fusion files
* Completed Goal 4: External Parts

## Decisions Made

* The legs and arms will remain static and cosmetic for V1
* The silicone rubber feet will carry the enclosure's weight and maintain stability
* The seated legs will use simple friction-fit square connectors rather than screws
* A 5 mm square leg interface was selected because an 8 mm version interfered with the faceplate area
* The arm mounting interface will use a compact square peg and socket rather than screws
* The left and right arms will share mirrored geometry to preserve symmetry
* Arms and legs will remain separate printable parts so they can be replaced independently

## Problems/Blockers

* The diagonal orientation of the original leg meshes made direct planar tracing unsuitable
* An initial 8 mm leg connector was too large for the available space near the faceplate
* The first leg socket depth caused coincident contact and was corrected from 2.5 mm to 2.7 mm
* The arm loft initially produced an unsatisfactory transition because of the hand's existing fillet
* Removing the early hand fillet allowed the loft to transition more smoothly into the arm
