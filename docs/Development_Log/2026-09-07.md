# Project MO — Development Log

**Date:** September 7, 2026  
**Session focus:** Microphone selection and preliminary mechanical/electrical integration

## Session objective

Evaluate whether voice commands can be added to Project MO V1 and begin selecting a microphone from scratch, considering the existing ESP32 DevKit V1, display, speaker amplifier, power system, enclosure space, price, availability, and expected speech-recognition performance.

## Work completed

### 1. Voice-recognition architecture

- Confirmed that the selected DOIT ESP32 DevKit V1 can capture microphone audio while continuing to control the Nextion display, MAX98357A amplifier, speaker, and physical buttons.
- Chose Wi-Fi-assisted speech recognition as the preferred V1 architecture.
- Retained the physical buttons as an offline fallback.
- Defined a push-to-talk sequence in which speaker playback is muted while the ESP32 records a short voice command, reducing feedback without requiring acoustic echo cancellation.

### 2. Microphone selection criteria

The earlier INMP441 entry was treated only as an initial possibility. The microphone was reconsidered from scratch using the following criteria:

- Compatibility with the exact ESP32 DevKit V1
- Electrical interface and GPIO use
- Compatibility with the available 3.3 V and 5 V rails
- Audio quality for speech recognition
- Physical dimensions and acoustic-port position
- Distance from electrically and acoustically noisy components
- Brazilian availability and price
- Breakout-board documentation and ease of installation

### 3. Mechanical placement

- Selected a location directly below the display and behind the existing horizontal faceplate slot.
- The slot, which previously provided additional ventilation, can now serve as the microphone opening.
- The location remains separated from the speaker/amplifier and ESP32 and does not obstruct the main cable paths.
- A future short acoustic duct or thin foam/rubber gasket may connect the microphone port to the faceplate slot and reduce internal speaker sound reaching the microphone.

The first rectangular microphone placeholder was accidentally created as **25 × 20 × 8 mm**. It was corrected to **20 × 10 × 8 mm** during the dimensional study.

After selecting the leading module, the Fusion 360 reference was replaced with a circular representation matching the advertised breakout-board size:

- PCB diameter: **14 mm**
- PCB thickness: **2 mm**
- Shape: circular, with two minor real-board edge notches conservatively ignored in the reference body
- Additional wiring clearance: approximately **4–6 mm**, to be represented separately if necessary

### 4. Interface decision

Digital I²S was selected as the preferred microphone interface because it:

- Avoids the ESP32 internal ADC's noise and calibration limitations
- Operates normally while Wi-Fi is active
- Supplies audio in digital form
- Can coexist with the MAX98357A using the ESP32's two I²S controllers
- Is well suited to 16 kHz mono voice-command recordings

Analog MAX9814 and MAX4466 modules remain fallback options, but they are not preferred because of ADC noise, gain configuration, and larger board dimensions.

### 5. Power decision

- The microphone should operate from the ESP32's **3.3 V rail**.
- No separate voltage regulator or logic-level converter is expected to be necessary.
- The selected module should draw less than approximately 10 mA and include local supply decoupling.
- A **0.1 µF ceramic bypass capacitor** should be located near the microphone supply pins; the exact breakout must be inspected to confirm whether it is already installed.
- A 3.3 V-only digital microphone must never be powered from the 5 V rail.

### 6. Preliminary GPIO allocation

The following provisional allocation confirmed that the ESP32 has sufficient pins and peripherals:

| Component | Signal | ESP32 GPIO |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Nextion display | RX from Nextion TX | 16 |
| Nextion display | TX to Nextion RX | 17 |
| MAX98357A | BCLK | 26 |
| MAX98357A | LRC/WS | 25 |
| MAX98357A | DIN | 22 |
| I²S microphone | BCLK/SCK | 14 |
| I²S microphone | WS/LRCLK | 27 |
| I²S microphone | SD | 34 |
| Seven physical buttons | Inputs | 13, 18, 19, 21, 23, 32, 33 |

This map remains provisional until the wiring diagram and firmware are created and tested.

### 7. Candidate comparison

The main locally obtainable candidates were compared:

| Candidate | Approximate listed price | Advertised dimensions | Result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| INMP441 | R$35.00 | Ø14 × 2 mm | Leading V1 candidate |
| SPH0645LM4H | R$89.00 | 16 × 11 × 5 mm | Higher-cost digital alternative |
| MAX9814 | R$23.90 | 25 × 13.8 × 9 mm | Analog fallback |
| MAX4466 | R$18.90 | 20 × 10 × 9 mm | Lowest-cost analog fallback |

The INMP441 offered the best overall balance of price, size, I²S compatibility, local stock, and expected speech quality. It was therefore selected as the **provisional V1 microphone**, pending inspection and physical testing of the purchased breakout.

## Decisions made

1. Add microphone-based voice commands to the intended Project MO V1 feature set.
2. Use push-to-talk Wi-Fi speech recognition rather than continuous listening.
3. Prefer a digital I²S microphone over an analog microphone.
4. Use the location below the display and behind the existing faceplate slot.
5. Use the ESP32 3.3 V rail for microphone power.
6. Adopt the circular INMP441 breakout as the current CAD reference.
7. Treat the INMP441 as provisionally selected, not fully verified, until the exact physical module is received and tested.

## Session outcome

The microphone integration is mechanically and electrically feasible. The INMP441 is the current leading candidate, and its circular Fusion 360 reference has been created in the proposed location. No additional voltage regulator is expected, and the ESP32 has sufficient GPIO and I²S resources. Final approval now depends on purchasing or obtaining the exact module and completing physical recording tests.
